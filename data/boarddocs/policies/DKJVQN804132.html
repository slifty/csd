Conduct on School Vehicles – Rules and Regulations

 

The rules and regulations that students are expected to follow while on school vehicles and at bus stops can be found by clicking on this link: Rules and Regulations - Cheltenham School District.

 

The Supervisor of Transportation or designee shall ensure, through adequate training of transportation personnel responsible for enforcing District rules, that such transportation personnel are familiar not only with the applicable student codes of conduct but also with all applicable Board Policy and Administrative Regulations regarding appropriate interactions with students.

 

Students are reminded that all school rules remain in effect when traveling on school vehicles and at bus stops. Violations of such rules and/or the rules/regulations may lead to school-based discipline in accordance with Board Policy and the applicable student handbook, which may include, but not be limited to, loss of privileges to ride on school vehicles.

 

When an infraction occurs, the school vehicle driver will fill out a School Bus Conduct report, listing the student’s name, school grade, bus #, date, whether the incident took place during the AM or PM run, and a description of the student’s conduct warranting the report. The bus driver contractor will then email a copy of the report to the school principal and/or designee and the District’s Transportation Office. The principal and/or designee and the Transportation Office will then start an investigation into the incident. An audio and/or video recording may be requested to complete the investigation.

 

Bus conduct reports should be submitted as soon as possible, so that the principal or designee and the Transportation Office are aware that an incident has occurred. If the incident happens on the PM run, the driver should at a minimum let dispatch know and submit a report no later than the conclusion of the driver’s AM run the following day.

 

 

Development and Selection of School Vehicle Stops

 

The Transportation Office is responsible for establishing school vehicle routes and the location of stops.  Bus routes, stops and schedules are designed with consideration given to safety, efficiency, lowest cost and overall ride time.  At the beginning of each school year, all students will receive the route number and stop location, along with a copy of the school bus rules and regulations.

 

Students may only get on/off the bus or other school vehicles at their assigned stop. All changes to stop assignments or locations must be approved in advance by the Transportation Office. Parents/guardians who feel a different stop is warranted for any reason shall contact the Transportation Office to request a change.

 

Hazardous Walking Conditions

 

If a parent/guardian believes that the walking conditions along their child’s route to and from school or to and from their child’s bus stop are hazardous, they may contact the Transportation Office in order to request a review of the walking area by the Transportation Supervisor or designee. If after the review, the Transportation Office denies the request, the parent/guardian may request, through the District, that the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation review the matter to make a final determination as to whether an alleged hazardous walking route constitutes a hazardous route. If the route designated to be hazardous is to the student’s bus stop, an alternate bus stop will be designated.

 

If the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation designates a route as hazardous pursuant to prescribed standards, the Board will provide the transportation for students who would otherwise walk to and from school but need to traverse the hazardous area.

 

Emergency Drills

 

The Supervisor of Transportation or designee  shall ensure, through adequate instruction and a minimum of two (2) actual drills each year, that every student is familiar with school vehicle emergency procedures, equipment, and safe loading and unloading operations, in accordance with law and regulations.

 

In accordance with applicable law, emergency drills shall be conducted on school vehicles as follows:

 

  1. All schools shall conduct two (2) emergency evacuation drills on school vehicles during each school year, the first to be conducted during the first week of the first school term and the second during the month of March, and at such other times as the Supervisor of Transportation or designee may require. Each drill shall include the practice and instruction concerning the location, use and operation of emergency exit doors and fire extinguishers and the proper evacuation of the vehicle in the event of fires and accidents.

 

  1. Bus operators shall be provided with proper training and instructions to enable them to carry out the provisions of law and may be required to attend classes and drills in connection therewith. Such training and instruction shall be provided by the bus operator’s employer.

 

Video and Audio Recording on School Vehicles

The Board has authorized the use of video and audio recording on school buses and vehicles while transporting students for school-related purposes for disciplinary and security purposes. The intention of video and audio recording on school buses and vehicles is to provide a safe environment for students, school personnel and contracted personnel. Audio and video recording devices are provided in select locations and do not provide comprehensive coverage of all school buses/vehicles.

Images and audio obtained from recording devices are for use by District administrative staff for investigation of student misconduct or other reports of conduct in violation of the law, school rules, or any applicable code of conduct, Board Policy, Administrative Regulation or collective bargaining agreement. Nothing in these Administrative Regulations or the accompanying Board Policy shall be deemed to supersede any provision of any applicable collective bargaining agreement.

Audio and/or video images that document misconduct involving a student, staff member, or other individual may be used at parent/guardian or staff conferences with District staff members, and in the case of any misconduct involving a possible criminal violation, in accordance with the District’s Memorandum of Understanding with law enforcement.

In the event that a student is disciplined for misconduct or is a victim of such misconduct and a recording is used as evidence to support such discipline or to document the occurrence of an alleged event, the parent(s)/guardians(s) of the disciplined student and/or victim may request permission in writing from the building principal to review the recording within one (1) week of the alleged incident.

If requested, a meeting will be arranged for the review of any applicable recordings with a designated District staff member, subject to the limitations of state and federal law, including, but not limited to, the Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA). The following rules shall be followed for viewing of and/or listening to video and/or audio recordings:

  1. Only the portion of the recording concerning a specific incident will be made available for viewing and/or listening with the exception of authorized personnel searching for images of a specific incident.
     
  2. Viewing of and/or listening to the recording will only be permitted on school property or as otherwise required by law.
     
  3. All viewing and/or listening will be in the presence of the building principal or designee.
     
  4. Copies of any recordings will not be provided, unless specifically required by law.

Aside from the narrow circumstances detailed above, these Administrative Regulations and the accompanying Board Policy creates no general entitlement for parents/guardians to view/review recordings obtained by the District in accordance with these Administrative Regulations and the accompanying Board Policy.

Recordings that document misconduct shall be preserved until at least the final disposition of any disciplinary proceedings, including the period of time during which a parent/guardian may request to view/review such recordings. Recordings will otherwise be erased on a regular basis in accordance with the District’s standard document retention policies and procedures.

Written notices as to the presence and possible activation of an audio recording of conversations concurrent with a visual image being taped must be posted on school buses/vehicles and in other locations where the audio recording will take place.

Regulations Regarding Diesel-Powered Vehicles

Pennsylvania's Diesel-Powered Motor Vehicle Idling Act prohibits any diesel-powered motor vehicle weighing 10,001 pounds or more to idle for more than five (5) minutes in any continuous sixty-minute period while parked, loading or unloading, except as allowed by law.

The District shall ensure that permanent signs, notifying drivers of the idling restrictions, are maintained on District property at locations where diesel-powered motor vehicles weighing 10,001 pounds or more load or unload. Signs shall also be posted at locations that provide fifteen (15) or more parking spaces for such diesel-powered motor vehicles.

Vehicles designated by the Diesel-Powered Motor Vehicle Idling Act ("Covered School Vehicles") include but are not limited to school buses. Generally, diesel-powered motor vehicles weighing 10,001 pounds or more are prohibited from idling for more than five (5) minutes in any continuous sixty-minute period. There are a number of exceptions specific to Covered School Vehicles, including but not limited to the following:

  1. When a Covered School Vehicle must idle to provide heating or air conditioning when non-driver passengers are onboard. In such case, the Covered School Vehicle may idle for no more than a total of fifteen (15) minutes in a continuous sixty-minute period, except when idling is necessary to maintain a safe temperature for students with special needs who are transported by a Covered School Vehicle. This exception does not apply to Covered School Vehicles that are waiting for students to board.
     
  2. When idling by a Covered School Vehicle off school property during queuing for the sequential discharge or pickup of students is necessary because the physical configuration of a school or the school’s surrounding street does not allow for stopping.
     
  3. When a Covered School Vehicle idles while forced to remain motionless because of on-highway traffic, an official traffic control device or signal or at the direction of a law enforcement official.
     
  4. When a Covered School Vehicle must idle to operate defrosters, heaters, air conditioners or cargo refrigeration equipment, or to install equipment, in order to prevent a safety or health emergency and not for the purpose of a rest period, or as otherwise necessary to comply with manufacturers’ operating requirements, specifications and warranties in accordance with federal or state motor carrier safety regulations or local requirements.
     
  5. When the primary propulsion engine of a Covered School Vehicle idles for maintenance, particulate matter trap regeneration, servicing or repair of the vehicle or for vehicle diagnostic purposes, if idling is required for that activity.
     
  6. When a Covered School Vehicle idles as part of a federal or state inspection to verify that all equipment is in good working order, if idling is required as part of the inspection.

 

Reporting Accidents

 

Any accident, regardless of the severity or circumstances, involving the operation of a school vehicle, with or without students on board, should be shall be reported immediately to the Transportation Supervisor by phone or text message.

 

The driver will contact dispatch and either the driver or dispatch will call 911 to notify the police department.

 

The driver shall remain at the site of the accident and wait for the police to arrive to fill out a report.

 

The Supervisor of Transportation or designee may require the driver to be tested for drugs and alcohol in accordance with the school bus company accident policy and procedure.

 

School Vehicle Driver Handbook

 

The transportation contractor should develop and maintain a driver’s handbook, which should contain instructions and directions for school vehicle drivers concerning transportation policies, regulations, rules, safety procedures, procedures for reporting accidents or other emergencies, and any other important instructions. This handbook should be updated as necessary.
