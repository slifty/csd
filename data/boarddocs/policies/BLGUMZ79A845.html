The District shall utilize multiple measures of academic performance as determinants in promotion and retention decisions.

Elementary & Middle School Students

 

In grades K-8, students who may be candidates for retention include, but are not limited to, those who fit into any one or more of the following criteria;

  • Are reading two years or more below grade placement;
  • Score at the Below Basic level on the reading and/or mathematics test in the Pennsylvania State System of Assessment (PSSA);
  • Are not earning a passing grade in two or more core subjects.

The parent(s)/guardian(s) of K-8 students being considered for retention shall be notified in writing by the school principal or designee upon a determination that their child is being considered for retention.  The principal shall invite the parent(s)/guardian(s) to an optional conference with the appropriate professional and administrative staff to explain the reasons why the student is being considered for retention.  

 

A student who is being considered for retention will subsequently be monitored and supported in order to have the opportunity to remediate the circumstances surrounding the student’s possible retention.  Prior to the end of the school year, the student’s parent(s)/guardian(s) shall be advised, in writing, if retention is ultimately recommended, and, if so, offer the parent(s)/guardian(s) a follow-up conference to further review the student’s academic progress and to discuss the recommendation for retention. 

 

The District’s decision with respect to retention or promotion shall be final.  Parents/guardians who disagree with the final determination may file a written objection addressed to the Superintendent, which will be placed and become a part of the student's academic record.

 

High School Students

 

In grades 9-12, placement of students is determined based on the student's ability to achieve the established academic standards and pass the required subjects and electives necessary to earn the number of credits mandated by the Board for graduation.

 

In order for a student to be a freshman, they must have successfully completed Grade 8.

 

In order for a student to be a sophomore, they must have completed at least 5.5 credits in courses required for graduation.

 

In order for a student to be a junior, they must have completed at least 11 credits in courses required for graduation.

 

In order for a student to be a senior, they must have completed at least 17 credits  in courses required for graduation. 

 

If a student is in danger of being retained, a notification will be sent to the student's parents/guardians prior to the end of the school year. The high school staff will meet with the student to inform them of their status and monitor their progress. Once a determination has been made, the student and parents/guardians shall receive written notice of the student's retention.

 

Special Considerations – IEP Students

 

If retention is being considered for an IEP student, the IEP team will be convened to review the student’s academic achievement; chronological age; and physical, emotional and social development. Academic achievement includes grades earned in the core subject areas, performance on standardized tests, and the level of proficiency demonstrated on the PSSA in reading and mathematics and Keystone Exams. The IEP team, in consultation with the Director of Special Education or designee, will determine whether the student is a candidate for retention.

 

Special Considerations – English Language Learners

 

Students who are identified as English Language Learners (ELLs) will not be retained solely based upon their lack of English proficiency.

 

If a student who participates in the English Language Development program is considered for retention, a careful review of the student’s academic progress will be conducted in consultation with the Assistant Superintendent or designee. The final recommendation for grade placement will be based upon this review.
