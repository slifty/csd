Purpose

 

A school counseling program is an integral part of the instructional program of District schools. Such a program can:

  1. Assist students in achieving their optimum potential.
  2. Enable students to significantly benefit from the offerings of their instructional program.
  3. Identify and assist with meeting intellectual, emotional, social and physical needs.
  4. Aid students in recognizing options and making choices in career and academic/educational planning.
  5. Assist students in identifying career options consistent with their abilities and goals.
  6. Help students learn to make their own decisions and to solve problems independently.
  1. Provide crisis intervention and response for the purpose of maintaining a calm and positive learning environment.
  2. Help integrate all the student's experiences so they can better relate school activity to life outside the school.

Authority

The Board directs that a program of school counseling shall be offered to all students in grades K through 12 that involves the coordinated efforts of all staff members, under the professional leadership of certificated counseling personnel.

Delegation of Responsibility

The Superintendent or designee is directed to implement a counseling program that serves the needs of students, consistent with applicable law and the guidelines below.

Guidelines

The District's program of school counseling shall:

  1. Be appropriately integrated with the instructional program at all levels of the District.
  2. Be comprehensive and developmental based upon tenets of the American School Counselor Association National Counseling Model framework.
  3. Utilize positive behavioral interventions and supports (PBIS).﻿
  4. Be culturally ﻿competent and aligned with the Board's Policies on Equity and Trauma-Informed Approach.
  5. Involve staff members at every appropriate level.
  6. Honor the individuality of each student.
  7. Be coordinated with services provided by locally available social and human services agencies, including, but not limited to, the District's social-emotional programming.
  8. Facilitate student interest in coursework and development of other skills to encourage, support, and prepare them for post-secondary education or other pursuits. ﻿
  9. Cooperate with parents/guardians and address their concern for the development of the student.
  10. Provide means for sharing information among appropriate staff members in the best interests of the student.
  11. Be available equally to all students.
  12. Establish a referral system that utilizes resources offered by the school and community, guards the privacy of the student, and monitors the effectiveness of such referrals.
