Enrollment and Registration Requirements and Verification of Residency

Registration Requirements
 

Except for students experiencing homelessness, foster care and other forms of educational instability, or students of active duty military families, as contemplated in the accompanying Board Policy, when a student of school age is presented for enrollment, District staff will require the following documentation, which may be submitted electronically, before enrolling the student and permitting them to attend school:

 

  1. Proof of the student's age. Acceptable documentation includes one (1) of the following:
    1. Birth certificate.
    2. Notarized copy of birth certificate.
    3. Baptismal certificate.
    4. Copy of the record of baptism, notarized or duly certified and showing date of birth.
    5. Valid passport.
    6. Prior school record indicating date of birth.
       
  2. Immunizations required by law. Acceptable documentation includes:
    1. Student's immunization record.
    2. Written statement from prior school district or a medical office that the required immunizations have been administered, or that a required series is being administered.
    3. Verbal assurances from prior school district or a medical office that required immunizations have been completed, with records to follow in a timely matter as required by law.
    4. Documentation of a valid exemption from immunization requirements, if the student is exempt.
       
  3. Proof of residency. Acceptable documentation typically includes one (1) of the following from “Section A” and two (2) of the following from “Section B,” provided address is clearly indicated in or on document and provided document is validly issued and genuine as to the resident presenting the document.

    However, particularly with respect to non-resident students permitted to attend District schools in accordance with Board Policy and Administrative Regulation 202, the District will be flexible in verifying residency, and will consider what information is reasonable in light of the student’s and family’s situation.

 

Section A.

  1. Deed.
  2. Valid lease. The District reserves the right to verify the validity of lease agreements directly with the landlord/property owner.
  3. Property tax bill.
  4. Settlement statement.
  5. Notarized multiple occupancy form, verified by a home visit by a designated D﻿istrict employee.
  6. Notarized student support affidavit form, verified by a home visit by a designated D﻿istrict employee.

 

Section B.

  1. Current utility bill.
  2. Current credit card bill.
  3. Current bank statement.
  4. PENNDOT identification or driver's license.
  5. PENNDOT vehicle registration.
  6. Copy of a state/federal program enrollment.
  7. Copy of current paycheck stub with name and address of employer as well as employee.

 

  1. Parent Registration Statement - a sworn statement attesting to whether (1) the student has been or is suspended or expelled for an act or offense involving weapons, alcohol, drugs, or infliction of injury or violence on school property, as required by law; or (2) the student was or is presently expelled for an offense involving a sexual assault conviction or adjudication, as required by law.
     
  2. Home Language Survey - for all students enrolling in a school for the first time. Enrollment of students may not be delayed in order to administer the Home Language Survey.

 

The District reserves the right to periodically confirm the status of students attending District schools.  This confirmation may include periodic visits to the home where the student is residing when conflicting information is provided or discovered regarding the eligibility status of a student.  Such referral and verification processes shall be carried out in a non-discriminatory and anti-racist manner.

 

Documents That May Be Requested But Not As A Condition Of Enrollment
 

District staff may ask for any of the following information, in addition to the required documentation, but will not require it as a condition of enrollment and will not delay a student's enrollment or attendance until the document(s) is provided:

 

  1. Picture identification.
  2. Health or physical examination records.
  3. Academic records.
  4. Attendance records.
  5. Individualized Education Program or other special education records.
  6. Completed physical examination.
  7. Completed Student Registration Form.

 

Documents That May Not Be Requested

 

District staff may not request any of the following information to verify enrollment or residency:

 

  1. Social security number.
  2. Reason for a student's placement if not living with natural parent.
  3. Visa of student or parent/guardian.
  4. Agency records. (Note: The District may still request verification that the child is residing with a foster parent or is in a pre-adoptive or adoptive home in the form of a letter from the appropriate agency.)
  5. Court order or records relating to a dependency proceeding, except in limited circumstances that occur when a custody order, agreement, or dependency is being relied upon as the basis for enrollment.
  6. Student's immigration status.

 

Requests For Student Records

 

Upon enrollment of a student, District staff will contact the student’s prior school for a copy of the student’s educational records. The prior district, if within Pennsylvania, is required to forward the records within ten (10) business days of the date of request.

 

When a student transfers from a Pennsylvania school entity, District staff will contact the sending school for an official copy of the student’s disciplinary record. The prior district is required to forward the official copy within ten (10) days from receipt of the request.

 

Health records must be transferred from all public and private schools, upon the request of the building principal or designee.

 

Students Enrolling Without Prior School Records

 

If a student is presented for enrollment without prior school records or if a private school withholds an enrolling student’s records, the District may seek and accept information for a student placement that appears reliable as proof of successfully completed coursework, such as report cards and sworn affidavits of previous school teachers.

 

If reliable information cannot be obtained, the Director of Student Services or designee will promptly arrange for the evaluation of the student to determine the appropriate grade and/or courses for that student. The evaluation will consist of an interview and demonstration of the degree to which the student has achieved the academic standards established by the Board. The student and parent/guardian will be informed in writing of the results of the evaluation and the student’s placement.

 

Special Enrollment Considerations

 

Students Living with Resident Adult Other Than Parent

 

When a student lives with a District resident who is supporting the student without personal compensation (gratis), the student can enroll in a District school if the resident makes application and provides the required documentation.

 

In addition to the required documents, school staff will require the resident to provide one (1) of the following:

 

  1. Legal documentation to show dependency or guardianship which may include a custody or dependency order; or
  2. Sworn and notarized statement indicating that the signer is a District resident and is supporting the student without receiving personal compensation, the student is living with them continuously and not just for the school year, and the resident accepts all personal obligations relative to school requirements.

 

School staff may also require additional information to substantiate the sworn statement under (2) above before enrolling the student in District schools. 

 

A resident’s receipt of payments, such as Supplemental Security Income (SSI), Transitional Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), pre-adoptive support, child support, maintenance on public or private health insurance, support from the military or military personnel or other payments for or on account of the student will not be considered personal compensation or gain.

 

School staff will not require the natural parent(s) or former guardian(s) to provide information.

 

Foster Students

 

Non-resident students placed in foster care in the District are entitled to the same educational privileges as students residing in the District and all protections outlined under applicable law.  Non-resident special education students placed in foster care are entitled to receive an appropriate program of special education.  When a student is placed in foster care, or when a foster care student’s placement changes, and the District is determined to be the student’s school or origin, the District shall provide transportation to the foster care student in accordance with applicable law. 

 

In addition to the required documents, school staff may request a letter from the appropriate agency verifying that the student is residing with a foster parent or is in a pre-adoptive or adoptive home, but cannot require a court order or agency records.

 

Students Living in Institutions

 

If the District hosts a children’s detention home, drug and alcohol treatment center or similar facility, the District is required to provide an education, and special education when appropriate, to non-resident students placed in the institution.

 

Students placed in a children’s institution have the right to attend District schools if appropriate for the student, upon submission of required documents

 

Children of Military Personnel

 

When a student is a child of a Pennsylvania resident who is deployed by the military and the student is living for that time with relatives or family friends residing in the District, the student is entitled to attend District schools, upon submission of required documents pursuant to Section 13-1302 of the Public School Code.

 

School staff will presume the District resident is supporting the student gratis, without personal compensation or gain.

 

Students Returning from Delinquency Placements

 

When a student returns to the District from a delinquency placement, school staff cannot automatically place the student in an alternative education program just because they had been adjudicated delinquent.

 

A student returning from a delinquency placement is entitled to an informal hearing prior to being placed in an alternative education program in accordance with 22 Pa. Code §12.8 (c).

 

The purpose of the hearing is to determine if the student is currently fit to return to the regular classroom or if they meet the definition of a disruptive student. Factors the District will consider include:

 

  1. Whether the incident causing the adjudication occurred at school or a school-sponsored event;
  2. Student’s behavior in the placement;
  3. Recommendations of teachers and adults, such as the juvenile probation officers, who have worked with the student.

 

While the District shall offer to hold the informal hearing prior to placement in the program, if the student’s presence in the regular classroom poses a danger to persons or property or provides a disruption of the academic process, immediate placement in the alternative program may occur with the informal hearing to follow as soon as practicable.

 

Twins and Higher Order Multiples

 

Twins and higher order multiple siblings will be enrolled in the same manner as other students.  The Pennsylvania Public School Code provides for parental discretion in the classroom placement of twins or higher order multiples.  A parent or guardian of twins or higher order multiples who attend the same grade level at the same school may request that the children be placed in the same classroom or separate classrooms.  The parent or guardian must make the request no later than ten days after the beginning of the school year or ten days after the first day of the student’s attendance if the students are enrolled after the school year commences.  The District may recommend classroom placement to the parents/guardians and provide professional education advice to assist them in making the best decisions for their children’s education.  The District shall provide the classroom placement requested, unless, after consultation with the Superintendent or designee, the principal determines that alternative placement is necessary.  The law affords the parents/guardians the opportunity to appeal that denial.  A school district is not required to place twins or higher order multiples in separate classrooms if the request would require the school district to add an additional class to the grade level of the siblings.

 

Address Confidentiality Program (ACP)

 

Families enrolling a student using an Address Confidentiality Program (ACP) card which lists a post office box as their address will not be required to provide additional information regarding their place of residence. School records from the student’s former school will be forwarded through the ACP. Questions about a family’s eligibility for enrollment through the ACP should be directed to the ACP at 1-800-563-6399.

 

Other Considerations

 

Through an equity lens, District staff will consider what residency verification is reasonable in light of a family's situation and should be flexible.

 

When the required enrollment documentation is provided, the school staff will typically enroll the eligible student and permit them to attend school on the next school day after the student is presented for enrollment, but no later than five (5) business days after application.

 

The District cannot deny or delay a student’s enrollment based on the information contained in a disciplinary record or sworn statement.  However, the District can provide alternative education services during the period of expulsion for a student currently expelled for a weapons offense or for an offense involving a sexual assault conviction or adjudication. If a student has been expelled from the previous district for reasons other than a weapons or sexual assault offense, the District will review the student’s prior performance and school record to determine the services and supports that will be provided upon enrollment in the District.

 

Verification of Residency of Currently Enrolled Students
 

The administration may, at any time, require verification of continued residency of students from parents/guardians or other adults with whom they are residing in the District:

 

  1. On a District-wide or school-wide basis.
     
  2. Where the District reasonably suspects that the student may not legally reside in the District.

 

Such verification shall be in accordance with the Administrative Regulations accompanying Board Policy 202 (Eligibility of Non-Resident Students) and shall be carried out in a non-discriminatory manner.

 

Change of Address Procedures

 

Student's whose parents/guardians move within or outside of the District's boundaries are required to immediately, but in no event more than five (5) days after such move, notify the District's Registrar of such move and change of address.  This includes a move from one apartment to another within the same apartment complex.  

 

The District Registrar will require the parent/guardian to complete a Change of Address form and to submit certain documentation confirming the new address of the parent/guardian, if the new address is within the District's boundaries.  The District Registrar will require the parent/guardian to complete a Withdrawal Form if the new address is not within the District's boundaries.  

 

Parents/guardians are asked to provide the District with timely notification if their address changes, temporarily or permanently, to avoid unnecessary residency investigations and related proceedings.  

 

Enrollment Complaints

 

In the event a dispute arises regarding the enrollment of a student, the individual attempting to enroll the student may file a complaint, in writing, to the Superintendent. The individual may send written follow-up to the Department of Education, School Services Unit. The Superintendent or designee shall provide a written response to any properly filed complaint, along with a copy to the Department of Education, School Services Unit, if the complainant also filed their complaint there.
