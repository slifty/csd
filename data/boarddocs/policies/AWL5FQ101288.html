Extracurricular And Cocurricular Activities

District policy limits “participation in the district’s educational program, curricular activities, athletics, school organizations, student publications, or extracurricular activities” to “students enrolled in the district on a full-time basis unless participation is mandated by law.”

 

Examples of cocurricular activities, as defined in the accompanying policy, include activities such as the school newspaper, the marching band, and the television production studio.

 

Examples of extracurricular activities, as defined in the accompanying policy, include activities such as the robotics team, the ski and snowboard club, the ultimate frisbee club, and Students Against Destructive Decisions (SADD).


 

Participation in the District’s Educational Program

 

To the extent required by Section 502 of the School Code, students who reside in the district but who receive their education in a setting other than a public school may participate in district offerings for which they would be eligible if they attended school in the district, such as vocational schools, technical schools, and other educational departments.

 

Students Enrolled in Private Schools

 

Students enrolled in private schools may not participate in any District extracurricular or cocurricular activities.

 

Students in Intermediate Unit Placements, Approved Private Schools, Non-Traditional Placements, or Other Alternative Placements

 

Students in approved private schools, non-traditional placements, or other alternative placements who have been placed in these placements by the district are permitted to participate in the extracurricular activities and cocurricular activities offered by the district as if the students were attending district schools.

Such students may also participate in the district’s educational program or in cocurricular experiences if permitted or required by the student’s Individualized Education Program(IEP).

 

Students in Charter Schools

 

Students in charter schools have a right to participate in the district’s extracurricular and cocurricular activities if the charter school does not provide the same extracurricular or cocurricular activity.

Charter school students who wish to participate in such activities must meet the same eligibility criteria, or its equivalent, as students enrolled in school, meet the try-out criteria, or its equivalent, and comply with all policies rules and regulations of the governing organization of the activity. Eligibility and try-out criteria may include, but are not necessarily limited to, academic achievement and attendance requirements as well as any applicable physical fitness, height or weight requirements.

 

Charter school students may only participate in extracurricular and cocurricular activities at the school building the student would be assigned to if s/he were enrolled in the district.

 

While in school or at school activities, charter school students are responsible to meet the guidelines, as established by board policy, in the student handbook, and/or in classroom/course/activity procedures. They will be subject to the same disciplinary measures, grading, class, and attendance regulations as are required of fully enrolled students.

 

The district does not provide transportation for charter school students to or from extracurricular activities. Such transportation shall be the responsibility of the charter school student and/or his/her parents/guardians. If the district provides transportation to and from a contest, game, exhibition or event at another school and requires students to use the transportation provided by the district, charter school students likewise shall be required to use such transportation.

 

The district may charge the charter school for charter student participation in extracurricular activities, since these costs were part of the per-pupil payment that is paid to charter schools. This fee is on a “cost basis”, as determined by the district, for expenses such as uniforms, transportation, and miscellaneous costs.

 

Home-Schooled Students

 

Home-schooled students are permitted to participate in district activities such as clubs, musical ensembles, athletics (such as varsity sports), and theatrical productions unless the activity requires that a district class be taken for credit.

Home-schooled students who wish to participate in such activities must meet the same eligibility criteria, or its equivalent, as students enrolled in school, meet the try-out criteria, or its equivalent, and comply with all policies rules and regulations of the governing organization of the activity. Eligibility and try-out criteria may include, but are not necessarily limited to, academic achievement and attendance requirements as well as any applicable physical fitness, height, or weight requirements.

 

Home-schooled students may only participate in extracurricular and cocurricular activities at the school building the student would be assigned to if s/he were enrolled in the district.

 

While in school or at school activities, home-schooled students are responsible to meet the guidelines, as established by board policy, in the student handbook, and/or in classroom/course/activity procedures. They will be subject to the same disciplinary measures, grading, class, and attendance regulations as are required of fully enrolled students.

 

The district does not provide transportation for home-schooled students to or from extracurricular activities. Such transportation shall be the responsibility of the student and/or his/her parents/guardians. If the district provides transportation to and from a contest, game, exhibition or event at another school and requires students to use the transportation provided by the district, home-schooled students likewise shall be required to use such transportation.

 

If the district offers a physical exam or medical test as a condition of participation in the specific activity, then the exam or test will be available to home-schooled students. The dates and times of such physicals or tests shall be made available on the district's website or a general circulation publication.

 

Students on Homebound Instruction

 

Students who are receiving homebound instruction shall not participate in extracurricular activities sponsored by the district during the period of such homebound instruction, unless an exception has been granted by the Superintendent or designee.

 

Upon written recommendation of a licensed practitioner of the healing arts familiar with the student and approval of the Director of Student Services or designee, students transitioning into full-time school attendance will be permitted to participate in extracurricular activities sponsored by the district.

 

Students in Other Alternative Placements (Residential Treatment Facilities, Partial Hospitalization, Private Residential Rehabilitative Institutions)

 

These students may participate in academic programs and courses as if they were in private schools to the extent that their medical condition permits them. Unless the district makes the placement, they may not participate in extracurricular or cocurricular activities, unless the placement is of short duration and failure to allow participation could be construed as a violation of Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act.

 

Homeless Students

 

Students who are homeless, as defined in the McKinney-Vento Act and board policy and Administrative Regulation 259 (Homeless Students), are eligible to fully participate in extracurricular and cocurricular activities. The Superintendent or designee may waive policies, procedures, or administrative guidelines that create barriers for homeless students to participate in extracurricular and cocurricular activities.

 


Equal Access Act

 

The district shall provide secondary students the opportunity for non-curriculum-related student groups to meet on the school premises during non-instructional time for the purpose of conducting a meeting within the limited open forum on the basis of religious, political, philosophical, or other content of the speech at such meetings. Such meetings must be voluntary, student-initiated, and not sponsored in any way by the school, its agents or employees. Non-instructional time is the time set aside by the school before actual classroom instruction begins or after actual classroom instruction ends.

 

The meetings cannot materially and substantially interfere with the orderly conduct of the educational activities in the school.

 

The Superintendent or designee shall establish the length of sessions, number per week, and other limitations deemed reasonably necessary.
