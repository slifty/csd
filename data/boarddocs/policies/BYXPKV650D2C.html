Definitions

 

For purposes of this Administrative Regulation and the accompanying Board Policy, the following definitions shall apply:

 

Family physician shall mean either a doctor of medicine legally qualified to practice medicine and surgery in the Commonwealth, or an osteopath or osteopathic surgeon legally qualified to practice osteopathy or osteopathic surgery in the Commonwealth, who has been designated by the parent or guardian as the personal physician of the child.  To the extent that the Pennsylvania Department of Health permits additional healthcare providers to perform the duties contemplated herein, such healthcare providers will also be recognized by the District as being authorized to perform such duties.

 

Family dentist shall mean a doctor of dental surgery or dental medicine legally qualified to practice dentistry in the Commonwealth, who has been designated by the parent or guardian as the personal dentist of the child.  To the extent that the Pennsylvania Department of Health permits additional healthcare providers to perform the duties contemplated herein, such healthcare providers will also be recognized by the District as being authorized to perform such duties.

 

Guidelines

 

Prior to admission to school for the first time, a student's parent/ guardian shall present to the District certification from a primary care provider or vision screener that their child has passed or failed a vision screening performed by a primary care provider or a vision screening conducted by screeners with specific training in vision screening techniques and protocols approved by the Department of Health or has completed a comprehensive eye exam conducted by an ophthalmologist or optometrist within the previous twelve months.  This required screening is required by law to be obtained by the student’s parent/guardian, and is not a screening that is provided by the District. Individuals who are not able to obtain such a vision screening due to a financial hardship shall contact the building administrator or counselor of the school where their child is seeking enrollment so that the District can explore possible school or community resources that might be able to assist the family in obtaining the required screening.

 

Each student shall receive a comprehensive health examination upon original entry, in sixth grade, and in eleventh grade. This examination may be conducted by the school physician. A private exam conducted at the parents’/guardians’ request and at their expense will be accepted in lieu of a school examination, in accordance with the procedures as stated below.

 

 

Each student shall receive a comprehensive dental examination upon original entry, in third grade, and in seventh grade. This examination may be conducted by the school dentist. A private examination conducted at the parents’/guardians’ request and at their expense will be accepted in lieu of the school examination, in accordance with the procedures as stated below.

 

Place of Examination

 

The school physician and school dentist shall conduct medical, dental and other examinations in rooms set aside for this special purpose and equipped with adequate facilities and with such other accessories as may be required by the Secretary of Health for the thorough examination of children.

 

If facilities in schools are inadequate for any reason, including lack of privacy, for conducting medical, dental and other examinations, the District may, subject to the approval of the Secretary of Health, make arrangements for the use of laboratories and facilities of hospitals or clinics for examinations herein provided for.

 

Private Examinations

 

In lieu of a medical or dental examination conducted by a school physician or dentist, as described above, any child of school age may furnish the District with a medical or dental report of examination made at their own expense by their family physician or family dentist on a form approved by the Secretary of Health for this purpose. The in lieu examinations shall be made and the report shall be furnished prior to the date fixed for the regularly scheduled examination, but no earlier than one year prior to the opening of the school term during which the regular examination is scheduled.

 

Health Services

 

Each child shall be given the following:

 

(1) A vision test by a school nurse or medical technician in all grades through grade 5, and then at least every other year thereafter through grade 12. These screenings are in addition to the required vision screening of children that parents/guardians must obtain privately within the 12 months prior to their child’s admission to school for the first time.  Parents/guardians of a student who has failed a required visions screening shall be notified, and such student shall be required to undergo a comprehensive eye examination, performed by an ophthalmologist or optometrist, at the parent’s/guardian’s expense, as required by applicable law. Individuals who are not able to obtain such an examination due to a financial hardship shall contact their building administrator or counselor so that the District can explore possible school or community resources that might be able to assist the family in obtaining the required examination.

 

(2) A hearing test by a school nurse or medical technician to all kindergarten, first, second, third, seventh, and eleventh grade students.

 

(3) A measurement of height and weight by a school nurse or physical education teacher who shall use the measurement to compute a child's weight-for-height ratio. This measurement shall be taken on an annual basis for all Kindergarten through twelfth grade students.

 

(4) Tests for tuberculosis, upon initial entry into school, and in grade nine by a tuberculin test, if required by law ﻿or in the absence of a waiver of this requirement.  The District will also screen students who fall within a high-risk population for TB infection. 

 

(5) Scoliosis screening, which shall be given to all sixth and seventh grade students.

 

(6) Such other tests as the Center for Disease Control and Prevention’s Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP) may deem advisable to protect the health of the child.

 

All teachers shall report to the school nurse or school physician any unusual behavior, changes in physical appearance, changes in attendance habits and changes in scholastic achievement, which may indicate impairment of a child's health. The nurse or school physician or school dentist may, upon referral by the teacher or on his own initiative, advise a child's parent or guardian of the apparent need for a special medical or dental examination.

 

Recommendations as to medical or dental care shall be sent to each parent or guardian and to the family physician or family dentist on forms prepared or approved by the Secretary of Health with instructions to the parent or guardian to consult the family physician or family dentist and to notify the school authorities of the action taken with respect to the recommendations.

 

If a parent or guardian fails to report the results to the nurse or school physician, the nurse or school physician shall arrange a special medical examination for the child.  The parent/guardian shall be notified prior to any such special medical examination taking place. 

 

School physicians or school nurses shall inform teachers of the health conditions of students which may affect behavior, appearance or scholastic performance.

 

Parent/Guardian Notification

 

Parents/Guardians of students who are to receive physical and/or dental examinations or screenings shall be notified. The notice shall include the date and location of the examination or screening and notice that the parent/ guardian is encouraged to attend or may have the examination or screening conducted privately at the parent's/guardian's expense. Such statement may also include notification that the student may be exempted from such examination or screening if it is contrary to the parent's/guardian's religious beliefs.

 

Religious Exemptions

 

A student, or their parent/guardian if the student is a minor, who presents a statement that a medical examination is contrary to their religious beliefs shall be examined only when the Secretary of Health determines that the student presents a substantial risk to the health of others exposed to contact with the unexamined person.

 

Maintenance/Transfer/Destruction of Health Records

 

Health records shall be maintained for each child. These records shall be available in the school building where the child attends school and shall be available to the school nurse at all times. Such records shall be transferred with the child when they move from one school to another or from one district to another.

 

In the case of any child of school age who enrolls in the District and who previously attended school in another district in Pennsylvania, the designated District representative shall request that the district or school where the child previously attended to transfer the health record of the child.

 

A child's health record shall not be destroyed for a period of at least two years after the child ceases to be enrolled, but may be surrendered to the parent or guardian if the child does not re-enroll in an elementary or secondary school in Pennsylvania.

 

Notice Regarding Eligibility for Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP)

 

Each year, by August 15, the Department of Insurance is required to provide the District with notices detailing the existence of and eligibility for CHIP to provide to parents/guardians.

 

The designated District representative shall be responsible for providing such notice to the parent or guardian of each student enrolled in the District during the school year, upon receipt by the Department of Insurance.
