Guidelines

 

A person may not knowingly or willfully present any false information regarding the residency of a student for the purposes of enabling that student to attend District schools. A person may not knowingly enroll, attempt to enroll, or keep enrolled a student when the student is known to be ineligible for enrollment in the District.

 

Whenever credible information, complaints or other circumstances arise that raise doubt as to a student’s residency or continued residency, the Superintendent or designee may authorize an investigation to determine whether the student is in fact a resident of the District or otherwise entitled to attend District schools, in accordance with the procedures herein contained.

 

 

Upon a determination by the administration that a child is not eligible to attend school in the District, or if the District no longer has adequate proof of a student’s residence in the District, the student’s parent or guardian will be notified of such determination and the basis for such determination in writing. The District will request immediate proof of residency or that the student be withdrawn from the District and enrolled in the school district where they reside.  The notification will advise of the procedures for requesting a hearing before the Board or a duly appointed hearing officer to challenge the administration’s determination.

 

If, within the timeframe specified in the above-contemplated notice to the family, the District does not receive (1) adequate proof of the student’s residency in the District; (2) notification of the student’s withdrawal from the District; or (3) a request for a hearing to challenge the administration’s determination, the student will be disenrolled from the District in accordance with applicable law.

 

If the student’s parent/guardian timely requests a hearing to challenge the administration’s determination, a hearing shall be scheduled, notification of which shall be sent to the parent/guardian by certified mail, first class mail, and electronic mail (if available). The notification of hearing shall again include a Procedural Safeguards Notice—Charge of Non-Residency. The hearing date shall be set such that the parent/guardian has at least three (3) days advanced notice of the time, date, and location of the hearing, as well as the procedures and rights detailed in the Procedural Safeguards Notice—Charge of Non-Residency.

 

The hearing will be held before the Board of School Directors, an authorized Committee of the Board, or a duly appointed hearing officer for the Board.

 

If, upon official action of the Board of School Directors at a public Board meeting, it is determined that the child is not eligible to attend school in the District, the student will be dropped from the District’s rolls in accordance with applicable law. The parent or guardian will be charged tuition for the days that the child attended as a non-resident student, retroactive to the date it was determined that the child was not eligible to attend school in the District. The daily tuition rate shall be the most current rate established by the Pennsylvania Department of Education for the Cheltenham School District.

 

Under Pennsylvania law, a person is guilty of theft of services if they intentionally obtains services for themself or another which they know are available only for compensation, by deception or threat. This includes educational services. Further, a person commits a misdemeanor of the second degree, unsworn falsification to authorities, and can be required to pay a fine of not less than $1,000 if with the intent to mislead a public servant in performing their official function, they submit or invite reliance on any writing which they know to be forged, altered or otherwise lacking in authenticity or submits or invites reliance on any object he or she knows to be false. The District reserves the right to refer potential criminal activity to the Cheltenham Police Department and/or the Montgomery County District Attorney’s Office.

 

Consistent with the procedures outlined above, and in accordance with applicable law, the District may not dis-enroll a student on the basis of non-residency within the District's boundaries until:

 

  1. The parents/guardians or any other person having charge or care of the student have been provided an opportunity for a hearing to appeal the decision and any appeal process has been exhausted; or
     
  2. The parents/guardians or other person having charge or care of the student, after being notified of the opportunity for a hearing, decline to participate in the hearing process; or

 

  1. The parents/guardians or other person having charge or care of the child have been provided information from the District's liaison for homeless children and youth regarding the educational rights of homeless students under federal law in a manner and form understandable to the parents/guardians or other person having charge or care of the child; or

 

  1. A court enters an order directing the child to be disenrolled and enrolled in a different school.

 

Verification/Investigation of Student Residency

 

Whenever credible information, complaints or other circumstances arise that raise doubt as to a student’s residency or continued residency, the Superintendent or designee shall authorize an investigation to determine whether the student is in fact a resident of the District or otherwise entitled to attend District schools. Such an investigation could include:

 

  • Contacting the student or the student’s parents/guardians;
  • Contacting the parent/guardian’s employer or current landlord;
  • Contacting the current occupant of the parent/guardian’s residence;
  • Utilizing information provided by parents, students, teachers or community members to verify enrollment or residency documentation;
  • Home visits;
  • Surveillance or other investigation by District personnel or outside contractors;
  • Referral to the police or other appropriate law enforcement agency for investigation
    and/or prosecution, when approved by the Superintendent or designee.

 

The administration may, at any time, require verification of continued residency of students from parents/guardians or other adults with whom they are residing in the District

 

  1. on either a District-wide or school-wide basis; or
  2. where the District reasonably suspects that the student may not be legally residing in the District.

 

Guidelines for Waiver of Tuition for Financial Need/Hardship (Graduating Seniors Only)

 

As provided for in Board Policy, tuition may be waived for a Cheltenham High School student who has attended CHS for their entire sophomore and junior years whose family moves from the Cheltenham School District after the first school day of the 4th marking period of the student’s senior year in the event that the student or parent/guardian presents a compelling case of financial need/hardship to the Superintendent or designee. The burden of establishing a compelling case of financial need/hardship is on the student/family. The Superintendent or designee, with appropriate parent/guardian consent, may consider a student’s eligibility for free or reduced-price meals under the National School Lunch Program as one factor relevant to a determination of financial need/hardship.
